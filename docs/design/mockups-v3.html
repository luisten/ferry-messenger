Round 2 · Welcome

You asked for a complete redesign of Welcome. Here are three directions, all built from the new icon’s ferry. Each one says “Ferry”, keeps the three promises, makes “Connect Instagram account” the only primary action and keeps “Not affiliated with Instagram or Meta.” Motion is subtle, stops under Reduce Motion and never holds back the button.

A · The crossing Recommended

The name, then the picture that explains it: the icon’s ferry on a calm sea, carrying your accounts as two bubbles in their own colours. The swell drifts, the ferry rides it, and the bubbles come aboard once when the screen opens. Words and the button sit on the plain background, so the sea stays a picture and colour in the app still means account.

In one screenName, the crossing, two accounts, three promises, one button. Nothing behind a swipe.
BuildOne Canvas for the sea and the icon’s three shapes as SwiftUI Shapes, in a TimelineView. No assets, no dependencies.
RiskThe most motion of the three. It pauses under Reduce Motion, in Low Power Mode and when the app isn’t active, and never touches text or the button.

B · Postcards

The name at the top, then one card per promise in a pager, with the button always below. Each promise gets its own picture (the ferry, a reel playing in a chat, an empty sea). But two of the three promises are hidden until you swipe, people tend to skip pagers, and it costs three scenes instead of one.

C · Boarding pass

Typographic and nearly monochrome: a ticket for the crossing. From Instagram, to one inbox, via your own server. The passengers are your accounts, and the feed, Explore and the algorithm are left on shore. It explains the bridge better than A or B and needs no illustration. But a ticket is a joke you only enjoy once, and “From Instagram” on a ticket can read as an Instagram product.

Why A

  • It explains the name with the icon’s own ferry. Icon, launch screen and Welcome tell one story: the ferry you tapped is the one on the screen.
  • Everything is visible at once. The three promises and the button need no swipe. B hides two of the three.
  • It teaches the identity system before the first login: two accounts aboard, side by side, each in its colour with its mark.
  • The motion is ambient and cheap: one TimelineView at 30 fps, paused whenever it shouldn’t run. Nothing waits for it.
  • The sea stays a picture. Text and the button sit on the system background, so “colour means account” still holds for everything you read or tap.

A · Accessibility

  • Order: (1) “Ferry. All your Instagram DMs. None of the feed.”, one element with the header trait; (2) the scene, one image: “A ferry carrying messages across calm water.”; (3–5) the promises, one element each, glyphs hidden; (6) “Connect Instagram account”, button; (7) the legal line.
  • Dynamic Type: every text style scales. The wordmark stops at 62 pt. The scene keeps its marks at Large (.dynamicTypeSize(.large), as today) and shrinks to 70% (157 pt) at accessibility sizes. The button’s label may wrap to two or three lines (minHeight: 50). At accessibility sizes the legal line moves to the end of the scroll content.
  • Contrast: all text is on systemBackground in label or inkSecondary. Marks are white on account fills (5.18 : 1 or more).
  • Increase Contrast: no haze; swell lines at full foam. Reduce Transparency: nothing changes (no glass in the scene).

Round 2 · Icon in colour

You chose “Two aboard” with tint, so the icon is now in colour. That replaces round 1’s rule that the icon is monochrome. The new rule: the sea is Ferry’s colour and appears only as scenery (the icon, the launch screen and Welcome’s picture). Colour in the UI still means account. Three treatments of the same paths, each in light, dark and tinted at 120 pt, then at 60 pt among placeholder icons.

1 · Deep water Recommended

A white ferry on deep water. The tile is the sea, a petrol-blue gradient that is lighter at the top. The ferry and the first bubble are white; the second bubble and the wave are foam. In dark mode the colour moves onto the glyph, as Apple’s own icons do: a sea-blue ferry under white and foam bubbles.

At 60 ptIt reads as a boat first. White on the sea is 5 : 1 at the tile’s lightest point and 8 : 1 around the hull.
TintediOS drops colour there, so it matches round 1: ferry and front bubble take the full tint, the rest about half.
RiskA white glyph on a blue tile is Signal’s and Telegram’s family. A deeper, greener blue and a hull instead of a bubble or a plane keep it apart.

2 · Harbour

Round 1’s white tile with the glyph in sea colours: a deep-sea ferry and front bubble, a sea-blue second bubble and wave. It’s the closest to what you saw in round 1, and it opens into the white inbox with nothing to jump. But the colour lives only in the glyph, and a light tile fades into light wallpapers.

3 · Night crossing

A night sea: the tile goes from night blue to deep teal, with a pale sea-blue glyph. It’s round 1’s Tinted preview (pale blue on a dark tile) made the default, so if that preview is what “with tint” meant, this is the one. It’s quiet and elegant, but close to today’s black icon: it sinks into dark wallpapers, and its dark variant hardly differs from the default.

Side by side

The three on one Home Screen, in light and dark, among the same placeholders.

Why Deep water

  • It pictures the name: a white ferry on the sea. No other treatment says “ferry” as fast.
  • It holds up at small sizes (60 pt, and 40 pt in Spotlight and 29 pt in Settings): a white silhouette on a deep tile.
  • Its colour belongs to no account. The sea sits between Ocean and Teal and is darker than both, and it never appears as UI.
  • Dark mode follows Apple’s pattern (Messages, Mail): the colour moves from the tile to the glyph.
  • It’s ownable. The blue messaging icons are bright, flat and bubble-shaped; this one is deep, graded, and a boat.

Ruled out

  • A warm dusk (orange, pink and violet sky): that’s Instagram’s gradient, the one thing we must not resemble.
  • Bright sky blue (#0099FF to #2AABEE): Messenger’s and Telegram’s territory.
  • Sky above, sea below (a two-tone tile): at 60 pt the glyph splits in two, with dark bubbles above the horizon and a white hull below, and stops reading as one mark.
  • Account colours on the bubbles (Ocean and Ember): people choose their own colours, so a fixed pair would teach the wrong thing.

1 · App icon

SupersededYou chose A in colour: see “Round 2 · Icon in colour”. The monochrome files below stay for reference.

Four directions on the name: Ferry carries your messages across, from Instagram, through your own bridge, into one calm inbox. All four are monochrome (color means account, so the icon has none), built from plain paths, and checked at 60 pt next to neutral placeholder icons. A · Two aboard Recommended

A ferry with two bubbles on deck, side by side, over one wave. It says the name (a boat), the job (messages carried across) and the product’s one idea (several accounts, kept apart, travelling together). It keeps today’s two bubbles, so people who know the current icon still recognize it.

B · Afloat Runner-up

One bubble riding the water. The three dots say “on its way” and double as portholes. The boldest at 60 pt, but it’s a chat bubble first and a ferry second, and a three-dot bubble is a common chat glyph. It doesn’t say “several accounts”.

C · Two shores

Two bubbles on opposite shores, with a dotted crossing between them. A clear story at full size. At 60 pt the dots fade, and the picture reads as “sync” or “transfer”, which many apps already use.

D · Still water

A bubble on the horizon and its reflection: the calmest of the four. At 60 pt the reflection bars look like lines of text under a bubble, so it reads as “a message” rather than “a crossing”.

Why A

  • It explains the name. “Ferry” is unusual for a chat app; a boat makes it click at once. B, C and D need the name to explain the picture.
  • It carries the product’s idea: two accounts aboard one ferry, side by side, never merged. The account stack (section 3) now follows the same rule.
  • It stands out in a grid of bubbles. Messages, WhatsApp, Signal and Messenger are all bubbles; a hull is not.
  • Continuity: today’s icon is two bubbles. A keeps them and adds the boat.
  • Light by default. The default appearance is white, like the app’s light chrome, so the launch zoom from icon to launch screen is seamless. Dark mode gets the dark variant on iOS’s own backdrop. Today’s icon is black in both.

The thread header (contact name and AccountTag) sits on the scroll edge with no backing. When a photo, a video card or one of your own colored bubbles scrolls under it, the tag that says which account you’re in becomes unreadable, which breaks principle 2 exactly where threads are busiest.

#5 · The request bar still takes a quarter of the screen Medium

Open since the 14 Oct review (its #6): about 200 pt with 54 pt buttons, so a long first message leaves little history visible, and the request is where you most need to read before deciding.
