# Design review: Ferry (v3)

2026-10-15. The app formerly called IG Messenger is now **Ferry** (D15): it carries your Instagram DMs across, through
your own bridge, into one calm inbox. This pass covers the app icon, the launch screen, the inbox's account stack (the
user finds the overlapping circles annoying) and a review of the rest of the app. The mockups are in
[`mockups-v3.html`](mockups-v3.html); the recommended icon is [`ferry-icon.svg`](ferry-icon.svg),
[`ferry-icon-dark.svg`](ferry-icon-dark.svg) and [`ferry-icon-tinted.svg`](ferry-icon-tinted.svg).

- `scripts/make_icon.swift` and the AppIcon set;
- the account stack's code (`AccountStack` in `InboxView.swift`, `AccountAvatar` in `AccountIdentity.swift`).

**Captures.** The simulator was off limits (the code was being renamed and reinstalled). The pass uses the latest captures

## Summary

- **Icon: A, "Two aboard".** A ferry with two bubbles on deck, side by side, over one wave. It's monochrome and white by
  default. It explains the name, shows the product's one idea (several accounts carried together and never merged),
  and keeps today's two bubbles.
  - Runner-up: B, "Afloat" (one bubble with three dots riding the water). It's the boldest at 60 pt, but it reads as a
    chat app first and a ferry second.
- **Launch screen.** The inbox's background with the icon's glyph centred in a 120 × 120 pt image:
  - light: `#FFFFFF` with an ink glyph;
  - dark: `#000000` with a light glyph.

  No in-app transition after launch.
- **Account stack.** The accounts sit side by side, 4 pt apart:
  - each avatar is 30 pt (a 24 pt photo plus its ring), in a 44 pt glass capsule with 7 pt side padding;
  - up to three avatars show; from four accounts on, the first two and a "+N" disc;
  - a problem shows as a badge on the avatar of the account it belongs to;
  - widths: 44, 78 and 112 pt, never more.
- **Pass: 14 findings.**
  - **High:** #1, the thread header becomes unreadable when media scrolls under it.
  - **Medium:**
    - #2, the stack overlaps;
    - #3, group threads show their title twice;
    - #4, Welcome never says "Ferry";
    - #5, the request bar is still about 200 pt.
  - **Low:** nine more.
- **Principle changes** (§5):
  - accounts sit side by side, never over each other;
  - the icon and launch screen are monochrome too;
  - identity in a toolbar sits on glass.

## 1. App icon

### Brief and constraints

- Theme: crossing; carrying across; a calm passage; a small boat or ferry; a bridge between two shores; two accounts
  carried together.
- Legible at 60 pt in the Home Screen grid.
- Light, dark and tinted variants (iOS 26).
- Nothing like Instagram's camera glyph or gradient, or Meta's marks.
- Buildable by `scripts/make_icon.swift`: plain paths and solid fills or one linear gradient. No filters, masks or text.
- DESIGN §0.1: color means account. The current icon is monochrome for that reason, and so is every concept here. A
  brand hue would compete with the account palette: Ocean, Teal and Slate are all "water" colors.

### Concepts

All four are drawn live in mockups-v3 §1, at 120 pt and at 60 pt among placeholder icons, in light, dark and tinted.

| | Concept | What it says | At 60 pt | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Two aboard**: a ferry hull (raked bow to the right, the direction of travel) carrying two bubbles side by side, over one wave | The name (a boat), the job (messages carried across), and several accounts kept apart but carried together | Reads as a boat with two bubbles; the wave stays visible (about 2 pt) | The most detailed of the four (four shapes). It must stay a silhouette: no portholes, no mast. | **Recommended** |
| B | **Afloat**: one bubble with three dots, riding two waves | A message on its way across the water; the dots double as portholes | The boldest and simplest | A chat bubble first, a ferry second; three dots in a bubble is a common glyph; nothing says "several accounts" | Runner-up |
| C | **Two shores**: two bubbles on opposite shores and a dotted crossing | The bridge between two places | The dots fade | Reads as "sync" or "transfer" | No |
| D | **Still water**: a bubble on the horizon and its reflection | Calm | The reflection bars look like lines of text under a bubble | Reads as "a message", not "a crossing" | No |

An earlier draft of A (a symmetric, bowl-shaped hull with two bubbles of the same size) read as a face: two eyes and a
smile. The raked bow, the rounded stern and the different bubble sizes fix that.

### Why A

1. **It explains the name.** "Ferry" is an unusual name for a chat app. With a boat in the icon, the name makes sense
   at once. B, C and D need the name to explain the picture.
2. **It carries the product's idea.** Two accounts travel aboard one ferry, side by side and never merged. The new
   account stack (§3) follows the same rule, so the icon and the UI tell one story.
3. **It stands out.** Messages, WhatsApp, Signal and Messenger are all bubbles; a hull is not.
4. **Continuity.** Today's icon is two bubbles. A keeps them and adds the boat.
5. **Light by default.**
   - The default appearance is a white tile, like the app's light chrome and the light launch screen. The system's
     zoom from the icon into the app then goes white to white, with nothing to jump.
   - Dark mode uses the dark variant on iOS's own backdrop, which zooms into a black launch screen.
   - Today's icon is black in both appearances.

### The files (recommended icon)

All three files are 1024 × 1024 and list their shapes in drawing order, with comments.

| Variant | File | Background | Ink: front bubble, hull | Second: back bubble, wave |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light (default) | `ferry-icon.svg` | linear gradient `#FFFFFF` (top) → `#E8E8ED` (bottom), opaque | `#16161A` | `#8E8E95` |
| Dark | `ferry-icon-dark.svg` | transparent (iOS draws its dark backdrop) | `#F4F4F6` | `#7C7C84` |
| Tinted | `ferry-icon-tinted.svg` | transparent | `#FFFFFF` (full tint) | `#8A8A8A` (about half) |

Geometry, in 1024 px units with a top-left origin:

| Shape | Where |
|---|---|
| Back bubble | 232 × 180 at (570, 216), corner radius 80, tail at the bottom right |
| Front bubble | 324 × 232 at (206, 252), corner radius 100, tail at the bottom left |
| Hull | deck y 572, x 162 to 862; keel y 700; raked bow on the right; rounded stern on the left |
| Wave | two periods from x 242 to 786 around y 794, amplitude 16, stroke 36, round caps |

The glyph's bounding box is about x 162–862 and y 216–830, centred on the tile with about 16% margin at the sides and 20% at the top and bottom.

**Rendering with `scripts/make_icon.swift`:**
- Map each SVG command to CoreGraphics:
  - `M` → `move(to:)`;
  - `H`, `V`, `L` → `addLine(to:)`;
  - `C` → `addCurve(to:control1:control2:)`;
  - `Z` → `closeSubpath()`.
- All coordinates are absolute, and the files use no arcs.
- Each bubble, tail included, is **one closed outline**, so there's no seam and no overlap to cancel out. Fill every
  path with the nonzero rule.
- The wave is a stroke: `setLineWidth(36)`, `setLineCap(.round)`, `setLineJoin(.round)`, then `strokePath()`.
- Keep the script's alpha rules: the light icon has no alpha channel (`noneSkipLast`); dark and tinted are
  `premultipliedLast` on a cleared context. Draw the light background with
  `drawLinearGradient(from: (0, 0) to: (0, 1024))`.
- The same paths also produce the launch image (§2).

**Look-alike check:**
- Instagram: no rounded-square camera outline with a lens and a dot, and no hue gradient.
- Messenger: no bubble with a zigzag. Our wave is smooth and sits outside the bubbles.
- Meta: no infinity loop.
- The bubbles have classic tails, not the paper-plane shape of Instagram's Direct icon. An early draft's separately
  drawn tails left a sliver that looked like a paper plane; single outlines fixed it.

## 2. Launch screen

iOS launch screens are static. The design is the inbox's background with the icon's glyph centred: on the user's
Home Screen, the icon zooms into a screen of the same color and mark, then the inbox replaces it.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Info.plist (`Config/Info.plist`) | `UILaunchScreen` = { `UIColorName`: `LaunchBackground`, `UIImageName`: `LaunchMark` } |
| Build setting | Set `INFOPLIST_KEY_UILaunchScreen_Generation` to NO (it generates an empty `UILaunchScreen`), so ours is the only one. **Verify** the built plist with `plutil -p`. |
| `LaunchBackground` (color set) | Any `#FFFFFF`, Dark `#000000`: the same as `systemBackground`, which the inbox uses |
| `LaunchMark` (image set) | **120 × 120 pt**: @2x 240 px, @3x 360 px, transparent. Any = `ferry-icon.svg` drawn without step 1 (the background rect). Dark = `ferry-icon-dark.svg` as is. Render both with the icon script at 240 and 360 px. |
| Placement | Centred by iOS, with no safe-area offset. The glyph covers about 82 × 72 pt of the box, twice its size on the Home Screen. |

```xml
<key>UILaunchScreen</key>
<dict>
    <key>UIColorName</key><string>LaunchBackground</string>
    <key>UIImageName</key><string>LaunchMark</string>
</dict>
```

**Transition after launch: no.** Ferry opens to the inbox, usually with chats already cached:
- A logo animation would delay, on every open, the one thing people opened the app for.
- iOS already cross-fades from the launch screen to the first frame.
- `RootView` has no loading state of its own: the first frame is the inbox, or onboarding.
- If one is ever added, it must be a 0.2 s opacity fade of the glyph only, skipped with Reduce Motion and never
  blocking input. I don't recommend it.

**Alternative:** a plain background with no image. This is Apple's preference ("look like the first screen"), and it
feels slightly faster. I chose the mark because during the rename it's the one moment that says "Ferry" on every
launch. Choosing the plain background means dropping `UIImageName`; nothing else changes.

**Verify on device:** iOS caches launch screens. Delete the app (or restart the simulator) before checking a change.

## 3. The account stack, without overlap

### What's there today

`AccountStack` (`InboxView.swift`):
- `HStack(spacing: -5)` of up to three `AccountAvatar(size: 26, showsMark: false)`, with `.padding(.horizontal, 4)`.
- `AccountAvatar` draws its ring **outside** its frame (`.padding(-2 * line)`): a 1.5 pt gap and a 1.5 pt line. Each
  avatar therefore looks 32 pt wide while taking 26 pt of layout.
- Avatar centres are 21 pt apart, so the photos overlap by **5 pt** and the rings by **11 pt**.
- A status glyph (an orange triangle, or a spinner) follows the stack with 11 pt of leading padding. It doesn't say
  which account it's about.
- The capsule measured 75 × 44 pt for two accounts (REVIEW-2026-10-14). The system's toolbar glass adds about 20 pt
  of width to the 55 pt of content.

### Options

| | Option | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Side by side with a gap, and a count from four accounts on** | **Recommended.** Calm, every account visible up to three, bounded width. |
| B | Four tiny avatars in a 2 × 2 grid inside a 44 pt circle | No. At about 17 pt per photo, nobody recognises their own avatar, and the rings stop reading as colors. |
| C | Account marks (colored letters) instead of photos | No. It repeats the chips directly below, and loses the personal recognition of photos. |
| D | Show only the scoped account (all of them in All) | No. The button would change size with every scope change, and in an account scope it would hide another account's problem. |

### Spec

| Part | Value | SwiftUI |
|---|---|---|
| Avatar | 24 pt photo + 1.5 pt gap + 1.5 pt ring in the account fill = **30 pt** | `AccountAvatar(…, size: 24, showsMark: false).frame(width: 30, height: 30)`: the frame now includes the ring |
| Spacing | **4 pt** between rings | `HStack(spacing: 4)` |
| Capsule | 44 pt tall, **7 pt** side padding (equal to the vertical margin, (44 − 30) / 2) | `.padding(.horizontal, 7).frame(height: 44)` + `.glassEffect(.regular.interactive(), in: .capsule)`. The `ToolbarItem` gets `.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden)`, so the system doesn't add its own capsule and padding (**verify** on iOS 26.4). If you keep the system glass instead, drop our padding and expect about 20 pt more width. |
| Widths | 1 account **44** (a circle, like the other glass buttons) · 2 → **78** (today 75) · 3 → **112** · 4 or more → **112** | |
| 4+ accounts | The first two in Accounts order, then a 30 pt disc "+N" (N = count − 2): `Color(.tertiarySystemFill)`, `.footnote.weight(.semibold)` rounded, label color. It's monochrome because it stands for several accounts. | `let shown = all.count <= 3 ? all : Array(all.prefix(2))` |
| Status | A 14 pt disc in the background color at the avatar's bottom-right, **inside** its 30 pt frame, so it never reaches the neighbour. Inside the disc: `exclamationmark.triangle.fill` at 9 pt in `statusWarn` (needs you), or a 9 pt spinner (connecting). Needs-you wins over connecting. A hidden account's status goes on the "+N" disc. | `.overlay(alignment: .bottomTrailing) { StatusBadge(…) }`. It replaces the trailing glyph and its 11 pt padding. |
| VoiceOver | One element with the button trait: "Accounts: Alex, Alex Studio, and 2 more. Alex Studio needs attention." | `accessibilityLabel` built from names and health |

**Title fit:**
- With 3 or more accounts, the inline title has about 120 pt between the stack and the Folders button.
- "Chats" and account names fit, but a professional account's subtitle "@alexstudio · Primary" doesn't.
- Use `ViewThatFits` for the subtitle: "@handle · Primary", then "Primary". The handle is still in Chat Info and in
  Accounts.

**Why this works with the rest:** principle 6 ("account health is one glance away") now also says *whose* health. The
banner still names the account and the fix, and the stack's colors are the same rings as everywhere else.

## Smells noticed (separate issues, not changed)

- **Persisted `com.igmessenger.*` keys.**
  - The archive tag stores `com.igmessenger.archived_at` (`Inbox.archivedAtKey`) in room tags on the server. Renaming
    it without reading the old key would silently unarchive every chat.
  - DESIGN §3.1's planned `com.igmessenger.accounts` account-data type isn't built yet, so pick the final namespace
    before building it.
- **`AccountAvatar` draws its ring outside its frame** (`.padding(-2 * line)`), so layout never accounts for the ring.
  The stack's overlap was partly this: even at spacing 0 the rings would touch. The spec gives the stack a 30 pt frame;
  other uses (composer, Accounts rows, banner) may have the same hidden overlap with neighbours.
- **The start-of-thread header and the composer** both say which account you're using. Once the toolbar tag is
  readable again (#1), the start block may not need the tag at all. Revisit after #1 ships.

## Summary

- **Icon: Deep water.** A white ferry on the sea. The tile is a petrol-blue gradient (`#1A7893` → `#0A2E47`); the ferry
  and the front bubble are white; the back bubble and the wave are foam (`#9ED6E2`). In dark mode the colour moves to
  the glyph: a sea-blue ferry and wave (`#2F9CC5`) under white and foam bubbles.
  - Harbour (round 1's white tile, glyph in sea colours) is the calm alternative.
  - Night crossing (a night-blue tile, pale-blue glyph) is round 1's Tinted preview made the default. If that preview
    is what "with tint" meant, pick this one.
- **The new rule (replaces principle 10): the sea is Ferry's, and only scenery.** The brand colour appears on the app
  icon, the launch screen and the Welcome picture. It is never a tint, a control, a text colour or chrome. Colour in the
  UI still means account.
- **Launch screen:** the same layout, recoloured. Light: `#12536D` (the icon gradient's midpoint) with the white mark.
  Dark: `#000000` with the dark icon's glyph.
- **Welcome: A, "The crossing".** The wordmark "Ferry", the unchanged headline, then the icon's ferry on a calm sea
  carrying two account bubbles (A in Ocean, S in Ember), the three promises and the button. The swell drifts, the
  ferry rides it, the bubbles come aboard once. Everything you read or tap sits on the system background.
  - B, "Postcards": a three-card pager, one promise per card. It hides two of the three promises behind swipes.
  - C, "Boarding pass": a typographic ticket, from Instagram to one inbox via your own server. Clever once, gimmicky
    after, and "From Instagram" on a ticket can read as an Instagram product.

## R2.1 Icon in colour

### What changes in the rules

Round 1 kept the icon monochrome so that colour would only ever mean "account". The user wants colour. The way to
keep both: **the brand gets one colour, the sea, and the sea is never UI.**

| Where | Colour |
|---|---|
| App icon, launch screen, the Welcome picture | The sea (brand). Scenery only. |
| Everything you read or tap: text, controls, tint, chrome, banners, the Welcome text and button | Monochrome, plus account colours where an account is meant. Unchanged. |
| The sea's hue | Petrol blue, between Ocean (`#1F5FD6`) and Teal (`#0F766E`) and darker than both, so it never reads as one of the accounts. |

### Three treatments

All three use round 1's paths unchanged. Each is drawn live in mockups-v3 at 120 pt (light, dark, tinted), at 60 pt
among placeholder icons, and side by side on one Home Screen.

| | Treatment | Light (default) | Dark | At 60 pt | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deep water** | Sea gradient tile; white ferry and front bubble; foam back bubble and wave | iOS backdrop; sea-blue ferry and wave; white and foam bubbles | The strongest: a white silhouette on a deep tile (5 : 1 at the lightest point, 8 : 1 around the hull) | A white glyph on a blue tile is Signal's and Telegram's family. The deeper, greener hue and a hull rather than a bubble or plane keep it apart. | **Recommended** |
| 2 | **Harbour** | White → mist (`#E2EFF2`); deep-sea ferry and front bubble (`#0D3D5C`); sea back bubble and wave (`#2E8DB5`) | Pale ferry and front bubble; sea back bubble and wave | Clean, but the colour lives only in the glyph | Closest to round 1, which the user called bland; a light tile fades on light wallpapers | Alternative |
| 3 | **Night crossing** | Night gradient (`#1C2D44` → `#0B1A24`); pale-sea ferry and front bubble (`#A6DCEA`); sea back bubble and wave (`#3F91B3`) | Almost the same glyph on iOS's backdrop | Elegant and quiet | Close to today's black icon; sinks on dark wallpapers; dark and light barely differ | Alternative, if "with tint" meant round 1's Tinted preview |

**Ruled out:**
- **A warm dusk** (orange, pink, violet sky): that's Instagram's gradient.
- **Bright sky blue** (`#0099FF` to `#2AABEE`): Messenger's and Telegram's territory.
- **Sky above, sea below** (a two-tone tile): at 60 pt the glyph splits into dark bubbles above the horizon and a white
  hull below, and stops reading as one mark.
- **Account colours on the bubbles** (Ocean and Ember): people choose their own colours, so a fixed pair would teach
  the wrong thing.

### Why Deep water

1. **It pictures the name:** a white ferry on the sea. No other treatment says "ferry" as fast.
2. **It holds up small:** 60 pt on the Home Screen, 40 pt in Spotlight, 29 pt in Settings.
3. **Its colour belongs to no account** and never appears as UI.
4. **Dark mode follows Apple's own pattern** (Messages, Mail): the colour moves from the tile to the glyph.
5. **It's ownable.** The blue messaging icons are bright, flat and bubble-shaped; this one is deep, graded, and a boat.

### The files

1024 × 1024, the same geometry and drawing order as round 1 (REVIEW §1), one linear gradient, no filters, masks or text.

| Variant | File | Background | Front bubble | Hull | Back bubble | Wave |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light (default) | `ferry-icon-color.svg` | `#1A7893` → `#0A2E47`, top to bottom, opaque | `#FFFFFF` | `#FFFFFF` | `#9ED6E2` | `#9ED6E2` |
| Dark | `ferry-icon-color-dark.svg` | transparent | `#F4F9FA` | `#2F9CC5` | `#8FCBD9` | `#2F9CC5` |
| Tinted | `ferry-icon-color-tinted.svg` | transparent | `#FFFFFF` | `#FFFFFF` | `#8A8A8A` | `#8A8A8A` |

- **Four fills, not two:** in the dark file the hull no longer matches the front bubble, so `make_icon.swift` needs a
  colour per shape rather than an ink and a second colour.
- **Tinted is round 1's tinted, unchanged:** iOS drops colour there and tints by luminance.
- **Contrast:** on dark, the hull is 5.4 : 1 and the back bubble 9.5 : 1 against `#1C1C1E`. On light, the back bubble is
  about 4 : 1 against the tile behind it.
- **Look-alike check:**
  - no camera outline and no warm hue gradient (Instagram);
  - no bolt and no blue-to-pink gradient bubble (Messenger);
  - no loop (Meta);
  - no paper plane (Telegram, Instagram Direct).
- **Idea, not required:** iOS 26's Icon Composer (in Xcode, no dependency) could take the four shapes as layers (tile,
  wave, hull, bubbles) and give the icon Liquid Glass depth and a "clear" appearance for free. It would replace the
  PNG script. Worth a look once the colours are settled.

## R2.2 Launch screen, matched

|---|---|
| Info.plist | `UILaunchScreen` = { `UIColorName`: `LaunchBackground`, `UIImageName`: `LaunchMark` } (unchanged). **The trade-off.** In light mode the colour changes once, whatever we pick:
- **Recommended:** the sea icon opens into the sea launch screen (one continuous picture), then iOS cross-fades to the
  white inbox. That happens on cold launches only.
- **Alternative:** a white background with the mark in Harbour's colours (`#0D3D5C`, `#2E8DB5`). The hand-off to the
  inbox is seamless, but the sea icon opens into white.

The user asked for the launch screen to match the icon, and the zoom is the moment people look at the icon, so I chose
the sea. Switching is one colour value and one image.

Dark mode has no trade-off: the dark icon, the launch screen and the inbox are all black.

## R2.3 Welcome: three directions

| | Direction | What it is | For | Against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **The crossing** | Wordmark and headline; the icon's ferry on a calm sea with two account bubbles aboard; three promises; the button | Explains the name with the icon's own ferry; everything on one screen; teaches the identity system; one picture to build | The most motion, so it needs firm pause rules | **Recommended** |
| B | **Postcards** | Small lockup; a pager of three cards, one promise each with its own scene; dots; the button | Every promise gets a picture | Two of three promises hidden behind swipes; people skip pagers; three scenes to build | No |
| C | **Boarding pass** | Wordmark; a ticket (from Instagram, to one inbox, via your own server; passengers = your accounts; feed, Explore and algorithm "left on shore"); the button | Explains the bridge; nearly monochrome; cheap | A gimmick you enjoy once; "From Instagram" on a ticket can read as an Instagram product; a two-column ticket must stack at large text | No |

### Why A

1. **It explains the name with the icon's own ferry.** Icon, launch screen and Welcome tell one story: the ferry you
   tapped is the one on the screen.
2. **Everything is visible at once.** The name, the crossing, two accounts, three promises and the button need no swipe.
3. **It teaches the identity system before the first login:** two accounts aboard, side by side, each in its colour
   with its mark (§0.1, §0.2, principle 9).
4. **The motion is ambient and cheap:** one `TimelineView` at 30 fps, paused whenever it shouldn't run. Nothing waits
   for it.
5. **The sea stays a picture.** Text and the button sit on the system background.

### A: full spec

**Layout** (393 × 852 pt, Large text; top-down in a `ScrollView`):

| Part | Where | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | y 116, 57 tall | "Ferry", SF Pro Rounded Bold 48 pt via `@ScaledMetric(relativeTo: .largeTitle)`, capped at 62 pt (its AX1 size), tracking −0.5, `.primary`, centred |
| Headline | 8 below, 2 lines | `.title3` regular, `.primary`, centred, at most 320 wide, line break after "DMs." |
| Scene | 16 below, full width × 224 | See below. At accessibility sizes it shrinks to 70% (157 pt). |
| Promises | 28 below; 3 rows × 36, 16 apart | A 300-wide column, centred, rows leading-aligned. A 36 pt disc in `tertiarySystemFill` with the SF Symbol at `.body` (`tray`, `play.rectangle.on.rectangle`, `eye.slash`), then the text in `.body`. |
| Space | flexible | `Spacer(minLength: 24)`. On taller phones the extra height splits 1 : 1 above the wordmark and here (the content `VStack` has `minHeight` = the viewport). |
| Bar | button 732–782, legal 794–810 | `safeAreaBar(edge: .bottom)`, unchanged from today: button 50 tall, 20 side margins, `.glassProminent`, `.tint(.primary)`, `.labelOnTint()`; legal in `.caption` `.inkSecondary`, 12 below. At accessibility sizes the legal line moves to the end of the scroll content. |

The layout fits an iPhone SE (375 × 667) without scrolling: the wordmark starts at 44, the promises end at 549 and the
button starts at 581.

**The scene** (local coordinates, 393 × 224; back to front):

| Layer | Shape | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sky | Nothing drawn: the screen's `systemBackground` | `#FFFFFF` | `#000000` |
| Haze | 40 pt band ending at the horizon (y 132), clear → `seaHaze` | `#E4F0F3` | `#0B1C27` |
| Sea band | From the horizon to a wave edge at y 214 (amplitude 5, wavelength 131); vertical gradient `seaHorizon` → `seaFloor` | `#1A7893` → `#0D3F5B` | `#0F4561` → `#05192A` |
| Swell | Two sine strokes, 1.5 pt, round caps, at y 186 and 202; wavelengths 120 and 160, amplitude 2.5 | `foam` `#9ED6E2` at 50% and 35% | 40% and 28% |
| Wake | Two short 2 pt strokes from behind the stern, at the waterline | foam, 35–70% | same |
| Hull | The icon's hull × 0.3 (210 pt wide), deck 6 pt below the horizon, centred | `#FFFFFF` (5.1 : 1 on the horizon) | `#FFFFFF` (10.4 : 1) |

The bubbles use fixed sample identities, as today's illustration does, never the user's own.

**Motion:**

| Moment | What moves | Timing | Curve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrive, once per appearance | The ferry glides in from 28 pt to the left and fades in | 0 → 0.7 s | `.smooth(duration: 0.7)` |
| Board | Each bubble rises 12 pt from the deck, scales 0.6 → 1 and fades in, with its mark | front at 0.25 s, back at 0.40 s, 0.55 s each | `.spring(duration: 0.55, bounce: 0.3)` |
| Bob | The ferry and its bubbles, ±1.5 pt | 4.8 s period | sine of the timeline date |
| Roll | ±0.8° around the keel's centre | 6.4 s period, phase +1.1 rad | sine |
| Swell | Both lines drift left, so the ferry seems to move toward its bow; the edge drifts too | 8 and 5 pt/s; edge 3 pt/s; each wraps every wavelength | linear |
| Wake | Opacity 0.35 ↔ 0.7, the two strokes out of phase | 2.4 s | sine |

- **Never animated:** text, the promises and the button. The button is enabled on the first frame, and nothing waits
  for the entrance.
- **Paused:** under Reduce Motion, in Low Power Mode, when `scenePhase != .active`, and while a pushed step covers
  Welcome. At most 30 fps.
- **Reduce Motion:** no glide, no boarding, no bob, roll, swell or wake. The scene is drawn once at rest (a level
  ferry, bubbles already in colour) and appears with the screen.

**Accessibility:**
- **Order:**
  1. "Ferry. All your Instagram DMs. None of the feed." One element, header trait.
  2. The scene, one image: "A ferry carrying messages across calm water."
  3. The three promises, one element each, glyphs hidden.
  4. "Connect Instagram account", button.
  5. "Not affiliated with Instagram or Meta."
- **Dynamic Type:**
  - Every text style scales; the wordmark stops at 62 pt.
  - The scene keeps its marks at Large (`.dynamicTypeSize(.large)`, as today) and shrinks to 70% at accessibility sizes.
  - The button's label may wrap to two or three lines (`minHeight: 50`).
  - Mockups-v3 shows AX3.
- **Contrast:** all text is on `systemBackground`, in label or `inkSecondary`. Marks are white on account fills
  (5.18 : 1 or more).
- **Increase Contrast:** no haze; swell lines at full foam.
- **Reduce Transparency:** nothing changes (there's no glass in the scene).

**SwiftUI mapping:**
- `WelcomeView`:
  - `ScrollView` { `VStack(spacing: 0)` { Header, `CrossingScene`, `PromiseList` } }, with the content's `minHeight`
    equal to the viewport;
  - `.scrollBounceBehavior(.basedOnSize)`;
  - the bar in `safeAreaBar(edge: .bottom)`;
  - `.toolbar(.hidden, for: .navigationBar)`.
- `CrossingScene`:
  - `TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval: 1/30, paused: still))`, where `still` = Reduce Motion, Low Power Mode, or
    `scenePhase != .active`;
  - inside it, a `ZStack` of a `Canvas` (haze, band, swell, wake, all drawn from the timeline date) and the ferry;
  - the ferry gets `.offset(y:)` and `.rotationEffect(_:anchor: .bottom)` from the same date;
  - the entrance is ordinary `withAnimation` on an `@State arrived`.
- **New file `FerryShape.swift`:** one `Shape` per part (back bubble, front bubble, hull, wave), with the paths of
  `ferry-icon-color.svg` in 1024 units scaled to the rect. O4 and O6 reuse the hull and the wave.
- **Tokens (`Tokens.swift`),** with the comment "brand: scenery only":
  - `seaHorizon` (`#1A7893` / `#0F4561`);
  - `seaFloor` (`#0D3F5B` / `#05192A`);
  - `seaHaze` (`#E4F0F3` / `#0B1C27`);
  - `foam` (`#9ED6E2`).
- **Asset catalog:** a `LaunchBackground` colour set.
- **Copy:**
  - `welcome.title` keeps its words and gains a line break after "DMs.";
  - new `welcome.scene` (VoiceOver);
  - the other strings are unchanged.
- **Delete:** today's `WelcomeIllustration` and its `Drift` modifier.
